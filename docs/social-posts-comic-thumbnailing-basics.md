# Social Media Posts: Comic Thumbnailing Basics

## Twitter / X (Quick Tips & Engagement)
1. **Tweet 1 (Hook):** Never draw a final page without a plan. Thumbnailing is the secret step that saves you hours of redrawing and frustration. Learn the basics here: [Link] #ComicArt #Webcomic
2. **Tweet 2 (Tip):** The \"Z-pattern\" is your best friend. In Western comics, always arrange your panels and speech bubbles so the reader's eye naturally flows left-to-right, top-to-bottom. #ComicTips
3. **Tweet 3 (Process):** Stick figures are perfect for thumbnails! Focus on the shapes, the composition, and the flow. Don't get bogged down in drawing the perfect eye when you're just planning the page. #MakingComics

## Instagram (Visual Storytelling)
4. **Carousel Post:**
   - Slide 1: "Why your comic page feels stiff..." (Text over a confusing layout).
   - Slide 2: "You skipped the thumbnail stage!"
   - Slide 3: Show a rough, stick-figure thumbnail.
   - Slide 4: Show the final, polished page based on that thumbnail.
   - Slide 5: "Learn how to thumbnail like a pro. Link in bio!"
5. **Reel/Video:** A fast-paced timelapse screen recording showing a blank ComicFlow page being populated with 6 rough panels, quickly resized and arranged, then populated with basic asset silhouettes to test composition.
6. **Static Post:** A split image. Left side: A very rough, loose sketch of a 9-panel grid. Right side: The final inked page. Caption: "The blueprint vs. The final house. Don't skip the blueprint stage. Read our new guide on thumbnailing basics. [Link in Bio]"
7. **Story (Interactive):** Poll: "Do you sketch thumbnails before drawing your final comic page?" (Options: "Always!" / "Never, I just wing it.") Follow-up story linking to the guide for the "wing it" crowd.
